What is metal forming
formability?

Formability is the ability of a material to undergo the deformation demanded by a manufacturing process while remaining free of unacceptable defects.

Operational Definition (Process)
F = { process parameters | flow is stable & part meets spec }

Dependent on external conditions: tool kinematics, blank-holder force (BHF), friction (lubrication), punch radius, die radius, and drawing speed. It defines the "Process Window".

Intrinsic Definition (Material)
M = { material properties | sustains strain w/o localization }

Inherent capabilities dictated by: strain hardening exponent (n-value, limits necking), normal anisotropy (r-value, aids deep drawing), yield strength, and microstructure.

What determines the formability of steel sheets during metal forming?

Click to reveal the answer.

⟳

Answer

Formability is not determined by a single property; it depends on both material and process variables.

Material variables:

  • Yield strength: higher generally means lower formability.
  • Uniform elongation: higher means greater ability to deform before necking.
  • Strain-hardening exponent (n): higher means better resistance to localized necking and higher formability.
  • Plastic strain ratio (r-value): higher means better resistance to thinning, especially in deep drawing.
  • Anisotropy: greater directional differences can cause uneven deformation and affect formability.

Process variables:

  • Blank-holder force: increasing it can reduce wrinkling, but excessive force increases tensile stresses and may cause tearing.
  • Friction: increasing friction generally restricts material flow and increases the risk of failure.
  • Forming speed: increasing speed can alter deformation behavior and may increase or decrease formability depending on the material.
  • Tool geometry: larger punch and die radii generally reduce localized strains and improve formability.

Therefore, the formability of a steel sheet results from the interaction between its intrinsic properties and the conditions under which it is formed.
